# Use Me With Style Prototype Guide

Live demo: https://use-me-demo.vercel.app

This guide explains how to use the prototype, what Raisa can click, what each section demonstrates, and how to present the demo in a meeting.

![Storefront home](https://use-me-demo.vercel.app/guide/01-storefront-home.png)

## 1. Purpose Of The Prototype

The prototype is a strategic preview of the future Use Me With Style platform. It is not only a website mockup. It shows how the business could operate through one connected system:

- A premium storefront for customers.
- A private admin platform for Raisa and her team.
- Multi-market selling for Angola, Portugal, and international customers.
- AI-assisted marketing, support, analytics, and operations.
- A phased product direction that can start simple and grow into a more intelligent commerce platform.

The goal of the prototype is to help Raisa understand the product vision quickly. It gives her something tangible to click through, react to, and prioritize.

## 2. What Is Real vs What Is Simulated

This is a clickable visual prototype. It is built as a real web app, but the data is mocked.

Real in the prototype:

- The demo runs in the browser.
- Navigation works between storefront and admin.
- Product pages, cart, checkout, account, admin pages, and dashboards are clickable.
- The interface is responsive enough to communicate the intended product experience.
- The images in this guide are real screenshots from the prototype.

Simulated in the prototype:

- Payments are not connected to a real payment provider.
- Orders are not written to a database.
- Stock is not connected to a real inventory system.
- Customer data is fictional.
- AI suggestions are mocked to show the intended behavior.
- Meta Ads are not connected to the live Meta Marketing API.
- WhatsApp/Instagram/email support is represented as a product concept, not a live integration.

The right way to frame it is:

> This prototype shows the experience, workflows, and business logic we want to build. The first production phase would turn the most important parts into a real working platform.

## 3. How To Open And Navigate

Open the live demo:

https://use-me-demo.vercel.app

The top bar has three important controls:

- **Storefront / Admin**: switches between the customer shopping experience and Raisa's internal dashboard.
- **PT / EN**: switches the interface language.
- **Prototype label**: reminds the viewer that this is a concept demo, not the final production product.

Recommended first action:

1. Start in **Storefront**.
2. Show how a customer would browse and buy.
3. Switch to **Admin**.
4. Show how Raisa would manage the business.

## 4. Storefront Overview

The storefront is presented inside a mobile phone frame because the primary customer behavior is expected to be mobile-first, especially from Instagram, WhatsApp, and social campaigns.

The storefront demonstrates:

- Brand presentation.
- Collection discovery.
- Product browsing.
- Product detail.
- Size/color selection.
- Wishlist behavior.
- Cart and checkout.
- Multi-market selling.
- Customer account.
- Order history.

The visual style is intentionally premium and editorial. It should feel more like a fashion brand experience than a generic ecommerce template.

## 5. Storefront Home

The home screen is the first customer impression.

What Raisa can show:

- Hero area for the current collection or drop.
- Featured product presentation.
- Categories/collections.
- New arrivals.
- Editorial sections.
- Instagram-inspired shopping content.

What this communicates:

- The site can support launches, seasonal drops, and curated collections.
- Products can be merchandised beautifully instead of appearing only in a grid.
- The storefront can turn social attention into shopping action.

Suggested talking point:

> The homepage is designed to feel like the brand, not just a product list. It gives space for launches, collections, storytelling, and conversion.

## 6. Browsing And Product Discovery

From the storefront, Raisa can open browsing/category views.

Customers can:

- View all products.
- Filter by category.
- Search products.
- Sort or scan product cards.
- Open individual product details.

What this demonstrates:

- Customers who arrive from social media can quickly find what they saw.
- Customers can browse by mood, category, or product type.
- The catalogue can scale as more products are added.

Production implication:

- In the real platform, products would come from a database or CMS.
- Categories, tags, prices, stock, and product visibility would be managed from admin.

## 7. Product Detail Page

The product detail screen shows the main purchase decision point.

Customers can:

- View the product name and price.
- Select a size.
- Select a color.
- Add to cart.
- Favorite/save the product.
- See related products or complete-the-look suggestions.
- See waitlist behavior for unavailable sizes.

What this communicates:

- Fashion purchases depend heavily on size, color, availability, and confidence.
- The product page should reduce friction and answer the customer's key questions.
- Waitlist behavior can capture demand even when an item is sold out.

Suggested talking point:

> If a size sells out, the platform should not lose the customer. It should capture demand and tell Raisa what needs to be restocked.

## 8. Cart And Checkout

The cart and checkout flow demonstrates how a customer moves from interest to order.

Customers can:

- Review selected items.
- See quantities and totals.
- Continue to checkout.
- Enter customer/contact information conceptually.
- See payment and delivery options based on market.
- Receive an order confirmation.

Multi-market behavior:

- Angola can prioritize Kz, local delivery, and local payment behavior.
- Portugal can prioritize EUR and Portugal-specific delivery/payment flows.
- International customers can use an international market setup.

Production implication:

- Real payment integrations would be chosen during production planning.
- Shipping rules and payment options would depend on the operational model Raisa wants.

## 25. Roadmap

The Roadmap page explains the phased build direction.

Recommended framing:

- **Phase 1 - Launch**: storefront, catalogue, cart, checkout, admin essentials.

Why this matters:

- The full vision is large.
- Raisa does not need to build everything at once.
- Phasing keeps the first launch realistic.

## 27. Suggested Script

Short opening:

> I built this prototype to make the platform vision tangible. It shows both sides of the business: what customers experience when they shop, and what you would use internally to manage products, orders, customers, marketing, and growth.

When showing storefront:

> The storefront is mobile-first because most customers will likely arrive from Instagram, WhatsApp, or social campaigns. The goal is for the shopping experience to feel premium and simple.

When showing admin:

> The admin is where the platform becomes more than a website. It becomes a daily operating system for the business.

When showing AI:

> The AI features are designed to assist, not take control. They create drafts, suggestions, and alerts, while Raisa stays in charge of approvals and brand direction.

When closing:

> The first version should focus on the launch essentials. Once those are working, we can add automation, analytics, and AI in phases.
